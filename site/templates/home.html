What it is

Your address, say alice@example.com, points to a public key that example.com publishes and signs. Every message you send is signed with your key, so whoever receives it can check it really came from you. It's also encrypted on your device to their key, so the servers that carry it can't read it.

Finding someone works the way email already does, through DNS. There's no central directory, and no single company that a message has to pass through.

Two names, because they mean different things. DMCN is the network, meaning the deployments that actually exchange mail. DMCNP is the protocol they speak, and that is what this site specifies. You can implement it under any name you like.

Where it's up to. DMCNP works today, but it's a snapshot rather than a frozen standard. What that means

Want to see it running first?

dmcnd is a small reference server: one domain, one process, webmail included. It's there to read alongside the spec and to check your own implementation against. It isn't the protocol.
